definitions: under incomplete, "The fact in question is the one the finding's own question asks for", and a
rule that a finding raising two points takes the type of the first question either point answers yes. A third
assistant, starting fresh, was given the requirements and the findings with those definitions and with each
finding's Type, Also considered and Effort lines taken out (`blinded-findings-3.md` in this folder), and nothing
else, with the same instruction to apply the definitions strictly and assume nothing. It was agreed beforehand
that this would be the last retest whatever it found. Its table and remarks are copied here unchanged below the

Remarks on the definitions:

1. The Q3 test "no word speaks of it at all … however loosely" has no stated criterion for when a neighbouring word counts as speaking of the fact. This is what leaves F-11 open, and the first point of F-07 and second point of F-06 uncertain.
2. Q1 rests on "reasonable reading" without saying who judges reasonableness. F-02 flips between contradictory and vague on whether "complete" can reasonably mean "complete as far as retained".
3. The two-point rule lets one absent sub-fact pull a whole finding up to incomplete (F-07). The type then depends on how the reviewer worded the finding's question, not on the requirement alone.
4. Q2's first sentence ("no observation at all … however its words are read") and its second (a requirement that can be falsified but never verified) are different tests. A universal such as NFR-05 passes only by the second, so it would help to say that either one suffices.
