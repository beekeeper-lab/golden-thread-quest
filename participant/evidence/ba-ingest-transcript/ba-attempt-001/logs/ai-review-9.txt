## BLOCKING

**B1. A contact detail split across two cues is not redacted when the speaker's Zoom `Name:` label is not on the participant list.**
- SKILL.md section 8 claims this "holds whoever speaks each cue". It also gives `Please write to lee at example.` then `Dot com. Okay?` as an example that is redacted.
- Cause: an unlisted Zoom label stays in the words (section 5). The cues are read together with that label sitting between the two halves, as in `Call me on 317 555 Carol Guest: 0198`, so nothing matches.

OBSERVED with crafted input (ingest() called directly). Each of these left the detail fully visible, and the provenance file and report counted 0:
- unlisted `Carol Guest:` cues `Call me on 317 555` then `0198 after lunch.`;
- the same with a Zoom-style `Ada Example (Example Corp):` label;
- `3, 1, 7,` / `5, 5, 5,` / `0, 1, 9, 8.` across 3 cues;
- `lee at example.` / `Dot com. Okay?`;
- `my email is ada at` / `example.com`.

OBSERVED with a label longer than 60 characters: even when it is listed under `labels`, PREFIX_LABEL never reads it as a label, so the same leak follows.

OBSERVED on real data:
- The CLI on the real file trust_spanning_protocol GMT20250430, with both speakers listed by name (not with Zoom's added "(Org)"), printed "0 email address(es)".
- The record kept the dictated address visible in U-0037/U-0038, masked shape `A, ##. Aaaaa at gmail.` then `Dot com. Okay?`.
- Corpus scan: 14 cues where the label in the words blocks a cross-cue redaction. Without labels listed, the corpus gets 115 email and 20 phone redactions; with every label listed, 118 and 23.
- 4,216 of 9,139 distinct label uses have "(Org)". 22 files have a label over 60 characters, which can never be read as a label.

The test `a_detail_split_across_cues_is_redacted_in_each_utterance` uses only `<v Ada Example>` voice tags, so the Zoom form is never tried.

Smallest fix: for the read-together pass in redact_all, start each unlabeled cue after a leading `^[^:\n]{1,120}:[ \t]*` prefix. Then add split-cue tests with listed, unlisted, "(Org)" and over-60-character Zoom labels.

**M1 (medium). A rerun reports `unchanged` without checking the provenance file, even when it is empty, belongs to another record, or no longer matches the inputs.**
- **Did:** wrote a one-cue transcript, then emptied `sample.provenance.yaml` (wrote "" to it) and reran the same command. I also wrote a record, then reran after changing only what the provenance holds:

**M2 (medium). In the Zoom form, a speaker who is not on the participant list loses attribution, and the reason written in the record is false.**
- **Did:** Zoom cues `Pat: Can everyone hear me?` and `Carol Guest: Yes, loud and clear.`, and two dial-in callers `13175550100: I approve the budget.` and `13175550198: I object to the budget.`

**M3 (medium). Zoom labels longer than 60 characters are never read as labels, even when the person is listed. Real files contain them.**
- **Did:** listed a 64-character display name as a participant and ingested the cue `<that name>: We ship Friday.`
- **OBSERVED:** the cue becomes `Unattributed`, reason `no speaker label in the transcript`, with the name kept in the words. The voice-tag form of the same name is attributed and certain.
- **Real data:** 779 cues in 22 of the 1,665 Zoom files have a label of 61 to about 99 characters (counted with masked output only).

- **Fix:** `PREFIX_LABEL` is capped at 60 characters. Match listed names and labels by `startswith(name + ":")`, or raise the cap to the voice tag's 200. Have the meeting-file check warn about longer names.

**L1 (low). Control characters at a line end, or as a reference at the end of a cue, are silently dropped instead of refused.**
- **Did:** `<v Ada Example>Hello\x0c` + newline + `world&#11;`

### HIGH 1. A listed Zoom-form label longer than 60 characters is never attributed (unstated rule)
- **Input:** participant `{"name": "Ada Example (Example Foundation Working Group Liaison (EFWGL))", "role": "Guest"}` (62 characters). Cue text: `Ada Example (Example Foundation Working Group Liaison (EFWGL)): Hello there.`
- **OBSERVED:** the utterance is `Unattributed`, uncertain, with reason `no speaker label in the transcript`. The same name in `<v ...>` form is attributed and certain.
- **Contradicts §5:** "A label that matches a listed participant's name or one of their labels is attributed to that person." The limit comes from `PREFIX_LABEL` `[^:\n]{1,60}`. SKILL.md mentions 60 characters only for other-speaker lines and their known misses, never for the cue's own label.
- **Real data (OBSERVED):** 779 cues in 22 of the 1,665 cached Zoom files have their first colon past character 60. Masked shape: `Aaaaaa Aaaaaaa (Aaaaaaaaaa aaaaa aaa aaaaaaaa aaaaaaaaaaa (AAAA)): words`.
- **Effect:** conservative. The cue is marked uncertain, not given to the wrong person, and the words keep the name. Listing the long name does not help.
- **Smallest fix:** either raise the bound in `PREFIX_LABEL` (and `LINE_NAME`), for example to 200 as voice tags allow, or add this to §5: "A `Name:` label is read only when the name is at most 60 characters. A longer one, as Zoom writes some display names with an affiliation in parentheses, is not read even when listed, and its cue is `Unattributed`."

### MEDIUM 2. The meeting file's location is never checked
- **Input:** a meeting file at `<repo>/participant/meeting.json` with `"labels": ["Ada", "ada@example.com"]`, and a raw transcript outside the repository.
- **OBSERVED:** the run reports `written`.
- **The claim:** §2 says "Both files stay outside source control." Only the raw transcript is refused. §5 leads users to list guest email addresses as `labels`, and the script's own message says anything inside participant/ "would be handed in".
- **INFERRED:** contact details could be handed in, against the quest safety constraint.
- **Fix:** run `check_paths`' participant/ and git checks on `--meeting` too, or reword to "Keep both files outside source control; the skill checks only the raw transcript's location."

### MEDIUM 3. A `transcript_id` with a trailing line feed is accepted, so two records can share one ID
- **Input:** `"transcript_id": "tr-2026-10-21-probe\n"`, run while `probe.md` (ID `tr-2026-10-21-probe`) already exists in the folder.
- **OBSERVED:** `second.md` is written. Its front matter and provenance get a stray blank line, and its citation line splits across two lines.
- **Cause:** `TRANSCRIPT_ID` ends in `$`, which matches before a final `\n`. `front_matter_value` strips the stored ID, so the duplicate-ID check compares unequal strings.
- **Contradicts:** §2 (the ID must look like `tr-2026-10-21-short-name`) and §4 ("one transcript ID belongs to one record").
- **Same cause elsewhere:** `RECORD_NAME`. `--out .../x.md\n` wrote files named `x.md\n` and `x.provenance.yaml`, against §3's NAME rule. `DATE` is saved by `fromisoformat`.
- **Fix:** use `fullmatch` (or `\Z`) for `TRANSCRIPT_ID`, `RECORD_NAME` and `DATE`.

### LOW 4. A §8 known miss says something is missed that is actually redacted
- **Input:** `redact_all(["ada.lee@", "example.zz"])`.
- **OBSERVED:** `['[REDACTED:EMAIL]', '[REDACTED:EMAIL]']`, counted once. The spoken rule allows spaces beside `@`. Same result for `["ada", "@example.zz"]` and `["ada@", "example.co.uk"]`.
- **What really stays visible:** `["ada..lee@", "example.zz"]` and `["ada.@", "example.zz"]`.
- **Fix:** replace that clause with "a written address cut at its `@` whose name has two full stops in a row or ends in one, such as `ada..lee@` then `example.zz`".

### LOW 5. The output-path check is described as stricter than it is
- **Claim (§2):** the output check "compares how the paths are spelled. If `--out` spells the repository's folder differently from the script's own path, the run is refused."
- **OBSERVED as written:** `--out` through a symlink to the repository, through `participant/context/../context/transcripts/x.md`, and through `/tmp/...` instead of `/private/tmp/...`. Different capitals, an NFD folder name and `/System/Volumes/Data` are refused.
- **Fix:** "...it compares the paths after following symbolic links and `..`, so a path that spells the folder in other capital letters, another Unicode form, or through /System/Volumes/Data is refused."
- **Related:** a broken symlink at the record's own name is refused with the wrong reason, `--out must be a lower-case name` (`resolve()` follows it to `nowhere`).

### LOW 6. Some control characters are dropped, not refused
- **Claims:** §5 says a label that holds a control character once decoded is refused, for example `<v Ada&#13;Example>`. §9 says any Cc character is refused.
- **Labels, OBSERVED:** `<v Ada&#13;>Hi</v>`, `Ada&#10;: Hi`, `<v Ada&#31;>` and `Speaker 2&#11;: Hi` are all accepted. The label is read as `Ada` or `Speaker 2`.
- **Words, OBSERVED:** U+000B, U+000C, U+001C to U+001F and U+0085 at the end of a line, or at the start or end of a cue, are silently dropped. Example: line `x\x0b` gives `x`. A cue starting `\x0bAda: hi` is read as Ada.
- **Cause:** `str.strip()`/`rstrip()` count these as white space. A control character mid-line is refused.
- **Fix:** check with `has_control` before stripping, or say "a control character Python treats as white space at either end of a label, a line or a cue is dropped with the spaces".

### LOW 7. §9's refusal of control characters and bad references is broader than the code
- **OBSERVED as accepted:** `NOTE` + `a \x01 b`, `WEBVTT \x01`, a cue identifier `id\x01`, and `NOTE` + `a &#0; b`.
- **Why it matters little:** none of these is copied into the record.
- **Fix:** "...in a cue's words or speaker label".

### LOW 8. Speakers that differ only in capital letters are listed twice

### LOW 9. Upper-case `&#X` references are neither decoded nor refused
