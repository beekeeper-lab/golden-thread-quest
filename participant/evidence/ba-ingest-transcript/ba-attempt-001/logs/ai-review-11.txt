## BLOCKING (a privacy leak and a regression from round 2)

**1. An address or number split across Zoom-form cues stays visible when the second cue's next line holds any colon.**

- **Input:** ingest() with the usual test meeting (Ada Example and Ben Example listed, Carol Guest not listed) and two cues:
  - cue 1: `Ada Example: Write to ada dot lee`
  - cue 2: `Carol Guest: at example` + newline + `dot com before 10:30 please.`
- **OBSERVED, new code:** both utterances unredacted (`Write to ada dot lee` and `Carol Guest: at example\ndot com before 10:30 please.`), email count 0.
- **OBSERVED, old code:** `Write to [REDACTED:EMAIL]` and `Carol Guest: [REDACTED:EMAIL] before 10:30 please.`, email count 1.
- **Same leak with other tails (OBSERVED):**
  - A tail line of `dot com or see https://example.org` leaks the whole address.
  - Phone variant, cue 1 `Ada Example: My cell is 317` and cue 2 `Carol Guest: 555` + newline + `0100 after 10:30 works.`: `317` stays visible in the new code. The old code hid it.
- **What should happen:** SKILL.md 8 says a detail "that runs from one cue into the next … is replaced by its marker in each utterance it touches." No Known miss covers this. The nearest one is a `Name:` whose name holds a colon (`Carol Guest (Team: Ops):`), which is a different case.
- **Severity caveat.** Read literally, the new mechanism text ("whatever comes before a colon within the first 200 characters of a line") also leaves out `dot com before 10`. So you could call this HIGH (the documented promise is not kept) rather than BLOCKING. I rated it BLOCKING because an address round 2 hid is now fully visible.
- **Likelihood:** low. It needs a multi-line Zoom-form cue.
- **Cause (INFERRED from the code):** LINE_START_NAME counts any text before the first colon of a line as a Name:, so times, `https:` and `re:` all count. Round 3 also replaced round 2's reading (only each cue's leading Name: left out) instead of adding to it.
- **Suggested fix:** keep the round-2 reading as a third reading, counted unless an earlier reading found part of the detail. Also, do not treat text as a Name: when a digit follows its colon, which is the rule Section 5 already uses for `10:30`.
- **Fuzz evidence:** in 30,000 random multi-cue inputs, new showed more digits or address words than old in 9 cases. Every one I inspected had a non-name colon on a later line.
